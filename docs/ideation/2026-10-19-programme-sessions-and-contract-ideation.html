Sessions and the Contract

How the Programme pane could show each managed session, its next step, and the contract terms that step relies on.

19 October 2026Topic: programme-sessions-and-contractRepository study

Focus: sessions under PM management and their next steps; the operating contract as something consulted during work and amended with care.

Five directions, not a specification. Each keeps three acts apart: annotate (write a note), propose (draft a change), and approve (a prompt you type, cited in the journal). No click, pane gesture, worker message, or peer report becomes approval.

40 raw ideas → 21 distinct concepts + 4 combinations → 25 reviewed candidates → 5 survivors.

Codebase Context

A Programme has one driving PM session, which holds the space's lease. Workers have no record of their own: a worker is the owner of a work item. The PM keeps one "doing now" slot and a queue of next actions for the whole programme. No session has a next step of its own.

Recorded and shown in the pane todayRecorded but not shownNot recorded
Item owner pane and session label; per-item waits; queue entries {id, action, item, why}; contract terms as value and who set themOwner history per item; why, options, and change time for each term; journal beyond the last 8 eventsAny link from a next step to the term or rule that permits it; per-session next steps; past pane sightings; earlier term values

The contract today. Terms (remit, stop rule, autonomy, cadence, sources) keep only their current value. Proposing, accepting, and amending write journal rows; acceptance and amendment cite a typed prompt. Rules and autonomy live in a personal file.

Where the contract binds and where it does not. Code reads the stop rule, the remit, and the cadence. No code reads autonomy before the PM queues a next step. The rule matcher computes an autonomy level, and the caller discards it. The sweep consults the contract through written instructions only. After compaction, the PM cannot write until it re-acknowledges the rules. That acknowledgement is the only point where code makes the PM re-read the contract.

Liveness. The lease is live while the run's heartbeat is recent. "Orphaned" means the heartbeat is old, not that the process stopped. A wait counts as watched when someone is named as reporter, with no check that anyone is watching.

Past lessons in this repository: a scoped answer must not govern sibling work; a stated agreement is not the same as effective authority; command success is not progress. Outside prior art: air-traffic flight strips travel with the aircraft, and passing the strip is the handoff. Policy-as-code systems check boundaries at the moment of action. No source found shows a human–agent agreement that is inspectable, cited per decision, and amendable from the interface.

A separate broad study covered outcomes and proof, decisions, gates, next-move roles, and product moments. This study goes narrower.

Topic Axes

  1. Who holds a session — item owner, owner history, pane, driver lease, reporter and watcher.
  2. What moves next — the single doing slot, the queue, per-item waits, handed questions.
  3. Control passing and liveness — lease states, heartbeat-only liveness, handover, takeover, compaction.
  4. The contract as a working surface — terms, amendments, rules and autonomy, and how the PM consults them.

Survivors: two on the contract, one on each other axis.

Ranked Ideas

Start with the contract that cites and is cited. It closes the gap the evidence shows most clearly: the autonomy level is computed and then thrown away, so nothing connects what the PM does next to the term that allows it.

1. A contract that cites and is cited

Axis: The contract as a working surfaceConfidence 80%Complexity Medium

Each queued next step and the doing slot carry a small "because" label naming the contract entry they rely on, with its autonomy level, or "no clause recorded". Opening the label shows the clause text; it changes nothing.

In the other direction, each term card shows how many next steps cited it, which instructions narrow it, and how it takes effect: checked by code, written guidance only, or read by nothing. Today autonomy would show "written guidance only" on the queue path.

Basis
direct: programme_protocol.py:469 computes an autonomy level; _apply_match (programme_record.py:116–123) does not keep it. _h_queue (programme_record.py:614–637) stores {id, action, item, why, at} and reads no contract. A search of plugins/auto/lib/ found no code reading autonomy before a queue write. The stop rule and remit do have code readers. external: railway interlocking (a signal cannot clear without a set route) and legal citators (each statute lists the decisions that relied on it).
Rationale
You can see, per step, whether the PM acted under a term you agreed, and, per term, whether it governs anything. That turns the contract from a document read once into a record the work keeps citing.
Recorded / new
Recorded: the queue entry, the matcher's result at match time, term values. New: a citation field written when the PM queues a step; per-term citation counts; the binding label.
Downsides
Two autonomy structures exist. Action-keyed autonomy (adopt-autonomy <action>) is the natural link for a queue row's action. Rule autonomy is keyed by change kind, applies per item, and matched_rule is a list. Steps without an item have no matched rule. Pick the link deliberately. Keep the label warn-only: a "no clause" label that blocks the write would change enforcement, which belongs to the separate contract-design work.

2. The session strip

Axis: Who holds a sessionConfidence 80%Complexity Medium

The pane shows one strip per managed session, borrowed from air-traffic flight strips. Each strip carries who holds it (owner session, pane, and whether they agree), its owner history, its next line, its waits, and the slice of the contract that applies to it.

The next line is joined from queue entries whose item the session owns and is labelled as derived. Contract terms on the strip are marked own (instructions scoped to its item) or inherited (programme-wide). You can write a margin note on a strip; the note is an annotation and no approval path reads it.

Basis
direct: owner {pane, terminal_id, session_id} and owner history in item.sessions are written at programme_record.py:127–142 and not exported by programme_view.py. Instructions filter by applies_to at programme.py:141–150. The sweep flags a pane that reports a different session (programme_sources.py, around line 605). external: flight progress strips travel with the aircraft; controllers write on them.
Rationale
Today a worker exists only as an item's owner, and the pane lists items, not sessions. A strip per session answers "who is this, what are they on, what are they waiting for, and what may they do" in one place.
Recorded / new
Recorded: owner, owner history, waits, instructions, queue. New: exporting owner history; the queue-to-owner join; the margin-note record.
Downsides
Gaps in custody (spans with no owner or another session in the pane) cannot be drawn, because past pane sightings are not stored. The margin-note record is new data; it must stay unreadable by any approval verb.

3. Amendment with consequences

Axis: The contract as a working surfaceConfidence 60%Complexity High

The contract pane uses three inks: the clause (current value, who set it, when), a redline (a proposed change), and a margin note (an annotation with no force).

A redline shows the current and proposed values, the open items whose autonomy would change if the matcher ran under the proposal, and the words your typed approval must contain. Nothing submits or fills the prompt for you. Acceptance stays a prompt you type, cited in the journal.

Basis
direct: proposing writes agreement_proposed (programme.py:445); accepting writes agreement_accepted (:460); amending writes term_amended with set_by="shawn" (:478), citing a typed prompt. Refusal text already lists missing required words. The matcher is a pure function, so a dry run is plausible. external: maker–checker dual control in banking; policy changes as reviewable diffs.
Rationale
This is the "considered" reading of your request: a change to the contract shows what it would do before you agree to it, and the act of agreeing stays yours.
Recorded / new
Recorded: proposals, acceptances, amendments, required-word checks. New: the redline view, the dry-run consequence list, the margin-note record.
Downsides
Unverified: whether a mod can read an unsent prompt draft, and whether a dry run can merge rule layers from the personal file. A green checklist must never submit or auto-fill. Highest complexity of the five.

4. Contract read receipt and read-back

Axis: Control passing and livenessConfidence 75%Complexity Low–Medium

The prompt band shows one line: when the PM last re-read the contract and how many terms changed since. After compaction, handover, or takeover, the PM restates the contract in its own words, and the pane shows where that restatement differs from the record.

The restatement is a journaled note. It never clears the compaction lock and never grants authority.

Basis
direct: after compaction, write verbs refuse until rules --ack (programme.py:196–206), journaled as rules_acked. Amending a term does not set that lock. external: hospital I-PASS handover ends with the receiver restating the plan. Past lesson: a stated agreement is not effective authority.
Rationale
This is the "consulted during work" reading of your request. An acknowledgement proves a command ran, not that the PM holds the current terms. The receipt shows drift; the read-back shows misunderstanding.
Recorded / new
Recorded: ack rows, amendment rows. New: exporting ack time and per-term change time; the read-back note and its comparison.
Downsides
Comparing a free-text restatement to terms is approximate. Making an amendment raise the same re-read lock would change enforcement; that is a discussion for the contract-design work, not this view.

5. Waits that show whether anyone is watching

Axis: What moves nextConfidence 80%Complexity Low

Each wait shows one of three states: a live watcher (a pulse with its heartbeat age), a named reporter only (an open promise that ages), or a retry watcher queued but never started.

Today all three count as covered. Showing them apart tells you which waits will move without you and which depend on someone remembering.

Basis
direct: programme_predicate.py:78–80 treats a named reporter as watched with no liveness check. The sweep falls back to --who shawn --reporter shawn when no reporter fits. Retry watchers are queued as arm_retry_watcher and started by the PM by hand. external: immune "missing self" detection, where an absent signal is the alarm.
Rationale
A wait that silently depends on you is the most likely way work stalls unnoticed. This is cheap and uses data already recorded.
Recorded / new
Recorded: wait fields, watcher heartbeats, queued retry entries. New: only the display distinction.
Downsides
Small on its own. Changing what counts as "watched" for the stop rule would be a separate decision; this direction only shows the difference.
